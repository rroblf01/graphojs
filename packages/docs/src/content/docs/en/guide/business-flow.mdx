A "business" example: the full cycle of an order, from the customer's call
to their feedback, with **two real loops** (not just decorative ones), a
**failure state** that stops the process where it happens, and a progress
animation that fills in the path traveled up to the step you choose —
useful for tracking dashboards where you want to show "how far we've
gotten" in a process.

<Playground code={businessFlowExample} height={640} />

## What you're looking at

The diagram has **10 steps** on the main path (from "Customer calls" to
"Customer gives feedback"), plus a final decision node and two **loops**
highlighted in orange:

1. **Quality control → Manufacture product.** If quality control finds a
   defect, the process doesn't move on to "Ship to customer": it goes back
   to manufacturing. It's a short loop, contained within production.
2. **Satisfied? → Prepare contract.** If the customer isn't satisfied at
   the end, the process doesn't finish: the contract is prepared again from
   scratch. It's a long loop that reopens almost the entire process.

Click **🔁 Defect in quality control** or **🔁 Customer not satisfied** to
trigger each loop: the corresponding orange link blinks for a moment (so
you can see *which* loop was activated), and then the progress
automatically rewinds to the step it returns to — that step stays
completed, because that's exactly where the orange arrow points; only what
comes after it becomes pending again — with an explanation in the panel
below of what happened and why.

The color legend (pending / completed / failure / loop) is always visible,
not just after interacting.

### Simulate a failure

Pick a step from the **"Fail at:"** dropdown and click **❌ Simulate
failure**: the process advances (or rewinds) to exactly that step and
paints it **red** — it stops there, nothing after it gets colored on its
own, regardless of what step you were at before clicking. The panel below
explains which step failed and what's blocked. To recover, raise the
slider and click **▶ Animate**: the failed step is treated just like any
other pending step, so it completes (in green) and the process continues
normally — or click **↺ Reset** to start from scratch.

## How it works

- The model has **real cycles**: a link can go back to an earlier node
  without GraphoJS complaining — it doesn't require the graph to be
  acyclic. Each link is simply `{ from, to }`; the loop links additionally
  carry their own field (`loop: 'loopQuality'` / `'loopRetry'`) so they can
  be found later with `diagram.allLinks.find(...)` and colored orange.
- The slider **doesn't animate anything by itself**: it just stores the
  target step (0 to 10). The **▶ Animate** button calls
  `goToProgress(target)`, which looks at how many steps are completed
  *right now* and moves forward or backward from there — it never resets
  to zero. That's why chaining two buttons (or the slider and a loop
  button) always continues naturally from where it was, instead of jumping
  visually back to the start.
- Node colors are bound to the model
  (`new go.Binding('fill', 'fill')`), but the animation does **not** change
  them through `model.setDataProperty()` — it paints the rendered
  `Shape`/`TextBlock` directly (`node.panel.elements[0].fill = ...`) and
  calls `diagram.invalidate()`. The reason is a real side effect: *any*
  property change made through the model, even a purely cosmetic one like a
  color, triggers `Diagram.invalidateLinksForNode()` — which forces the
  route of **every** auto-routed link to be recalculated, not just the
  links of the node that changed. With this demo animating colors several
  times per second, that caused the long "Satisfied? → Prepare contract"
  link to be rerouted on every step, and sometimes the orthogonal router
  picked a different path that crossed over "Quality control" — visually
  mixing up the two loops. Painting the `Shape` directly changes the color
  without touching the model, so link geometry is never invalidated. For
  the links (their progress color, and the loop blink) the same thing
  happens for a different reason: they don't use binding, so it's enough
  to assign `link.stroke` directly.
- Each loop button always chains the same phases in the same order:
  1. `goToProgress(sourceStep)` — advances or rewinds to the step where the
     loop lives (e.g., "Quality control"), from the current progress.
  2. The loop link blinks, so you can see *which* one was activated.
  3. `goToProgress(targetStep)` — undoes step by step down to the step it
     returns to. That step stays completed (it's exactly where the orange
     arrow points); only what comes after it becomes pending again.
- `currentDoneCount()` counts how many consecutive steps, from the start,
  are green — and stops at the first step that isn't, whether because it's
  pending (blue) or **failed (red)**. That's why "Simulate failure" and
  "▶ Animate" work together with no extra code: the failed step counts as
  "not done," so retrying with the slider simply repaints it green and
  continues from there, like any other pending step.
- No animation piles up: clicking two buttons in a row (or the same one
  twice) invalidates a token from the previous animation before starting
  the next one.
